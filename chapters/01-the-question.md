  <h1>The Question</h1>

  <p>
    It started with a question that initially felt almost harmless.
  </p>

  <p>
    I was looking at what AI systems were becoming capable of doing.
    Not just answering questions or generating snippets of code, but
    increasingly understanding requirements, writing code, debugging it,
    testing it, and working across entire software projects.
  </p>

  <p>
    And then I started thinking about the people behind those tasks.
  </p>

  <p>
    Developers.
  </p>

  <p>
    QA engineers.
  </p>

  <p>
    DevOps engineers.
  </p>

  <p>
    Architects.
  </p>

  <p>
    Even managers.
  </p>

  <p>
    For years, these roles have represented careers that people spend
    years building toward.
  </p>

  <p>
    We learn programming languages. We learn databases. We learn cloud.
    We learn system design. We learn frameworks, tools, architectures
    and engineering practices.
  </p>

  <p>
    We spend our twenties trying to become better engineers.
  </p>

  <p>
    And now we have machines that can increasingly perform many of the
    things we spent years learning.
  </p>

  <blockquote>
    So I started wondering:
    <br><br>
    <strong>Is what I've learned becoming obsolete?</strong>
  </blockquote>

  <p>
    That question is uncomfortable.
  </p>

  <p>
    But I don't think avoiding it makes it disappear.
  </p>

  <p>
    The purpose of this book isn't to predict that AI will replace every
    software engineer. Nor is it to argue that everything will remain
    exactly as it is.
  </p>

  <p>
    Instead, I want to explore a more difficult possibility:
  </p>

  <p>
    <strong>
      What happens when the amount of work one person can accomplish
      increases dramatically because the person is working with AI?
    </strong>
  </p>

  <p>
    And if that happens, what does it mean for the people whose work
    becomes easier to automate?
  </p>

  <p>
    What happens to software teams?
  </p>

  <p>
    What happens to careers?
  </p>

  <p>
    What happens to the number of people companies need?
  </p>

  <p>
    And perhaps most importantly:
  </p>

  <p>
    <strong>What happens to an entire generation that is entering the
    workforce during this transition?</strong>
  </p>
