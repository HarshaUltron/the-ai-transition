Chapter 06

The India Question

The AI transition is not going to affect every country in exactly the same way.

And for India, the question may be particularly important.

India's technology industry has grown around a model that depends heavily on human knowledge workers.

Large teams of engineers, testers, analysts, support professionals and other specialists provide services to companies around the world.

The model has been enormously important to India's economic growth.

It has created careers for millions of people and helped create an entire ecosystem around technology employment.

The labour advantage

One of India's major advantages in the global technology industry has been the ability to provide skilled professionals at a lower cost than many Western markets.

Companies could distribute work across countries and use India's large engineering talent pool to operate software development, testing, infrastructure, support and other functions.

In simple terms, a company could often get more human labour for the same budget.

AI changes the equation.

If software becomes dramatically cheaper to produce because fewer people are required to perform the work, then the advantage of simply having a large pool of lower-cost labour may become less powerful.

What happens to IT services?

Imagine a project that once required 100 people.

If AI eventually allows the same amount of work to be completed by 50 people, the client has a choice.

They could use the additional capacity to build more.

Or they could simply spend less money.

If this happens across thousands of projects, the effect could become significant.

This doesn't mean India's technology industry disappears.

It means the economic model may have to evolve.

The impact goes beyond software engineers

There is another reason this matters.

High-paying technology jobs don't exist in isolation.

They support a much larger ecosystem.

Technology workers rent or buy homes.

They use restaurants, transportation, gyms, retail stores, delivery services and other businesses.

Companies occupy offices.

Buildings are constructed because people and businesses need them.

Entire neighbourhoods can develop around large employment centres.

When a large group of highly paid workers becomes economically uncertain, the effects can spread far beyond the original industry.

This is not uniquely an India problem

Every country facing large-scale automation will have to deal with similar questions.

But India's situation deserves particular attention because the technology services industry plays such an important role in the country's economic story.

A transition that reduces demand for large numbers of routine knowledge workers could therefore affect not only individual careers, but also the structure of the industry itself.

The opportunity

There is also a very different possibility.

India could use AI to become dramatically more productive.

Instead of competing primarily through lower-cost human labour, Indian companies could compete through highly capable AI-assisted teams.

A small team that can deliver the work of a much larger team could potentially serve more customers, build more products and enter markets that were previously too expensive to pursue.

AI could therefore become an enormous productivity opportunity.

But productivity alone doesn't answer the employment question.

The transition matters

If new opportunities appear, people need to be able to move into them.

That requires training.

It requires time.

It requires companies to invest in people.

And it requires the economy to create enough new opportunities to absorb people leaving declining roles.

Saying "people will learn new skills" is therefore only the beginning of the conversation.

The harder question is:

Will the economy create enough valuable work for the people who are displaced by increasingly capable machines?

India's next advantage

Perhaps India's next advantage will not simply be having more engineers.

It may be having engineers who can use AI exceptionally well while understanding complex business domains, systems and customers.

The competitive advantage could shift from the quantity of labour to the quality and leverage of talent.

That would be a significant change.

And it is one worth preparing for before the market forces everyone to prepare for it.
